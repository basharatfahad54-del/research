Structure of a research proposal in Engineering Management (PhD/M(RD)) 
1. Title (Cover) Page
2. Abstract: A concise summary (250-300 words) outlining:
•
Research problem
•
Objectives
•
Methodology
•
Expected contributions
Note that writing the Abstract is the final step of your proposal writing! 
3. Introduction:
•
Background and context
•
Problem statement
•
Research aims and objectives
•
Research questions and/or hypotheses
•
Justification of the study
4. Literature Review: Recent accredited peer-reviewed articles, and other accredited peer-
reviewed publications (full conference papers, book chapters, books) 
•
Review of relevant theoretical, empirical, and review studies
•
Identification of knowledge gaps (Using gap spotting strategy)
•
Positioning of the study in the existing body of knowledge
5. Theoretical Framework:
•
Explanation of the importance of the theoretical framework in the PhD research,
supported by relevant references (This is a general discussion and not specific to your
topic)
•
Explanation of the main theories or models underpinning your research
•
Explanation of how these theories relate to your research problem
•
Justification for the selection of particular theories: Explain how these inform your
research questions and expected findings
Note: Engineering Management often operates at the intersection of technical systems and 
organisational behaviour. Therefore, the theoretical framework helps you ground your inquiry 
in academic theory (e.g., Technology Acceptance Model, Lean Thinking, Complexity Theory). 
The conceptual framework shows how you, as a researcher, interpret the real-world problem 
in a structured, researchable way. 
6. Conceptual Framework:
•
A visual and narrative representation of the key variables, constructs, and their
presumed relationships


=== PAGE BREAK ===

•
Reflects your understanding of how the research elements interact
•
Can be derived from both the literature review and the theoretical framework
Tip: Include your own well-labelled diagram showing the flow of influence, feedback loops, 
or process dynamics in the system being studied. 
7. Research Design and Methodology
•
Research paradigm and approach
•
Research strategy (case study, survey, action research, etc.)
•
Data collection methods and instruments
•
Sampling and participants
•
Data analysis methods
•
Validity, reliability/trustworthiness
•
Ethical considerations
•
Delimitations and limitations
8. Context and Case Study (if applicable)
•
Description of the empirical setting
•
Relevance to Engineering Management
9. Expected Contributions
•
Theoretical contributions
•
Practical implications
•
Policy or sector-specific insights
•
Methodological innovations
10. Research Plan / Timeline
11. Preliminary Chapters Outline
12. References:
•
Use a reference manager (EndNote) to auto-format citations.
•
Cross-check all in-text citations against the bibliography.
•
It is prohibited to use AI tools to generate or rephrase text. In addition you 
must not use theses tools to generate and cite fake references.
•
Your references must be traceable through their DOI links.
•
Use only accredited sources from UJ databases. Do not use predatory or non 
peer reviewed sources.
