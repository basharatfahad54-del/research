# Research Topic Analysis

**Topic (fixed):** *Developing an Innovative Systemic Living Lab for Just Energy Transition in a South African Informal Settlement.*

---

## 1. Anatomy of the topic

The title contains five load-bearing constructs. Each one commits the study to a particular literature, and each must be operationalised before Chapter 3 can be written.

| Construct | What it commits the study to | Where the existing draft stands |
|---|---|---|
| **Developing** | A design-and-development study, not a purely explanatory one. The output is an artefact/framework (the Systems Living Lab) plus the knowledge generated in building and testing it. | Present: DRM (Blessing & Chakrabarti 2009) is used and a biogas pilot is claimed. |
| **Innovative** | A defensible claim of novelty relative to existing Living Labs and existing energy-innovation practice. Novelty must be located in a specific mechanism, not asserted. | Weak: novelty is claimed as "first SETILL", but no systematic comparison against existing energy Living Labs is offered. |
| **Systemic** | Systems thinking as method *and* ontology — interrelationships, feedback, boundaries, emergence, multi-level structure. | Partly present: SsT/SE, system dynamics and causal loop diagrams are discussed, but "systemic" is never defined against "systematic". |
| **Living Lab** | A real-world, multi-stakeholder, co-creative experimentation environment with users as co-creators, iterated over time. | Present and reasonably well reviewed (definitions, typology, cases, scaling). |
| **Just Energy Transition** | Normative commitment to distributive, procedural and recognition justice in the shift of energy systems. | **Absent.** "Just transition" and "energy justice" appear zero times; the draft substitutes "gender equality". |
| **South African informal settlement** | A bounded empirical context with specific historical, infrastructural and institutional conditions (apartheid spatial legacy, municipal grid, informal tenure, prepaid metering, paraffin/wood use). | Present, but split between Tzaneen (South Africa) and Umuahia (Nigeria). |

**Analytical consequence.** The topic as now worded is a *justice* study delivered through a *systemic Living Lab*, in a *specific national context*. The draft is a *gender* study delivered through a Living Lab in *two countries*. The centre of gravity has moved, and the thesis must be re-centred rather than patched.

---

## 2. The problem the study addresses

The brief's problem statement can be decomposed into four claims, each of which needs its own evidence base:

1. **Widespread energy poverty and insecurity** in South African informal settlements, disproportionately affecting poor households.
2. **Exclusion of end-users** from the design, development, prototyping and testing of energy technology innovations, so that innovations do not match end-user utility requirements, cultural practices or affordability.
3. **Weak acceptance and uptake** of energy innovations, attributed to a combination of user-side factors (fear of change, incomprehension) and structural factors (political, institutional, economic).
4. **A justice deficit** — the absence of greater representativity and agency in the design of energy systems — which is treated as both a cause and a symptom of energy insecurity.

The existing draft states this in gendered terms and attributes the deficit to the absence of *women* from design. The supervisor's brief reformulates it as the absence of *end-users*. That reformulation is not merely lexical: it changes the unit of analysis from a social category (women) to a role in a socio-technical system (end-user), and it therefore changes what counts as evidence.

---

## 3. Research aim

> "To advance theoretical and empirical knowledge of the development of energy technology innovations within a systems-thinking Living Lab that would at least address the positioning of energy acceptance at the core of the research, design, creation, prototyping and testing of energy innovations, in order to ensure the end-users' utility requirements are met in ways that would address energy poverty."

The aim contains **two deliverables**: a theoretical/empirical contribution (knowledge about how such a Living Lab works) and a practical one (a framework that positions acceptance at the centre). It also contains an implicit causal claim — that placing acceptance at the centre of development *produces* better energy-poverty outcomes — which the study must test rather than assume. This is where the conceptual framework has to do real work, because the draft's implicit logic ("participation → acceptance → energy security") is a chain of contingent, not necessary, relationships.

---

## 4. The five research questions: what each one demands

| RQ | Object of inquiry | Type of question | Theory needed | Method implied |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RQ1** — How do Systems Living Lab concepts frame the relationship between inequality, energy security, energy poverty, energy technology innovations and informal settlements? | Conceptual framing | Conceptual/theoretical | Systems thinking, posthumanism, energy justice | Conceptual analysis + systematic review |
| **RQ2** — Which factors determine the involvement and participation of end-users in decision-making, co-designing, co-creating, co-researching, prototyping and testing? | Determinants of participation | Explanatory (qualitative) | Posthumanism (agency/distributed agency), participatory innovation, socio-technical theory | Interviews, workshops, stakeholder mapping, thematic analysis |
| **RQ3** — Which cultural elements, norms, practices and technological mechanisms influence acceptance? | Acceptance formation | Explanatory (socio-material) | Posthumanism (materiality, intra-action), social practice theory, TAM/UTAUT | Ethnography, observation, interviews, survey |
| **RQ4** — Which mechanisms can improve acceptance and end-user contribution to energy security services? | Intervention design | Design/prescriptive | Living Lab theory, systems thinking, energy justice | Co-design workshops, system dynamics, Delphi/validation |
| **RQ5** — Can the proposed Systems Living Lab engage end-users and stakeholders in real-world settings to address energy poverty and enhance energy security and access? | Evaluation of the framework in practice | Evaluative | Systems Living Lab, posthumanism | Pilot/case study, mixed-method evaluation |

**Observations that matter for Chapter 3:**

- RQ1 and RQ5 are the *bookends*: RQ1 is conceptual, RQ5 is evaluative. The theoretical framework must be able to do both — explain and evaluate. Posthumanism alone is stronger on the first than the second, which is precisely why supporting perspectives (energy justice, Living Lab theory) are needed.
- RQ3 is the only question that is explicitly about **acceptance**, and the aim makes acceptance central. Yet "acceptance" is the least theorised construct in the draft. It appears as adoption, uptake, take-up, acceptance and acceptability, interchangeably. Chapter 3 must distinguish (a) acceptance as attitude, (b) acceptance as use, (c) social acceptance (Wüstenhagen et al. 2007 distinguishes socio-political, community and market acceptance), and (d) acceptance as a negotiated socio-material achievement.
- RQ4 asks for *mechanisms*, which is a realist question (what works, for whom, under what conditions). That is a different logic from RQ1's framing question, and the theoretical framework must be explicit about handling both without collapsing into mechanism-talk that posthumanism would resist.
- None of the five questions mentions gender. The theoretical framework must therefore explain energy vulnerability *without* relying on a gender ontology, while not misrepresenting the sources that do.

## 5. The gender → "end-users" instruction: an analytical problem, not an editing task

This is the highest-risk instruction in the brief, and it must be handled deliberately.

**The scale of the problem.** The draft contains 505 occurrences of "gender" and 335 of "women". More importantly, gender is not decoration in the draft — it is the explanatory mechanism. The problem statement, the aim, all four objectives, the literature review's three-level architecture, the conceptual framework's stakeholder logic, the case-study sampling and the claimed novelty ("gender design") all rest on the claim that energy poverty in informal settlements is *gendered* and that the justice deficit is a *gender* deficit.

**Why find-and-replace would fail.** Substituting "women" with "end-users" would produce sentences that are empirically false or conceptually incoherent, for example:
- "Women bear the brunt of energy poverty because they fetch fuel" → "End-users bear the brunt ... because they fetch fuel" (destroys the causal claim).
- "More women live in poor informal settlements" → "More end-users live in poor informal settlements" (tautology).
- References to SDG 5, the Gender Development Index, the Gender Inequality Index, gender mainstreaming and the "equality stool" (Booth & Bennett 2002) cannot survive substitution at all.

**A defensible strategy (recommended, subject to supervisor approval).** Three tiers:

1. **Retain and re-frame.** Where a cited author's argument *is* about gender (Musango et al. 2020; Anditi et al. 2022; Kovacic et al. 2019; Cecelski 2012), retain the finding and report it accurately as a *dimension of differentiation among end-users*, then subordinate it to the study's end-user framing: "end-users are not homogeneous; gender is one axis of differentiation, alongside tenure, income, household structure and dwelling type." This preserves the literature without making gender the study's organising category.
2. **Replace with role language.** Where the draft uses "women" to mean "the people who cook, fetch fuel, and make household energy decisions", replace with "end-users" or "household end-users" — because in that sentence the referent really is a role, not a sex.
3. **Delete.** Where gender is an additive aside that carries no argumentative weight (e.g. claims that gender diversity improves organisational performance), delete. The brief explicitly permits removing irrelevant gender discussion.

**What must not be done.** Do not delete gender from a cited author's argument in a way that misrepresents it, and do not retain SDG 5 as a pillar of a study that no longer studies gender. Either the study keeps a gender dimension (as a differentiated finding) or it drops it (and then SDG 5 leaves the framework). A half-position will be caught at examination.

**Consequence for the title and framing.** If gender is removed as the organising category, then "just energy transition" must carry the normative weight that gender equality previously carried. That is why energy justice theory becomes structurally necessary rather than optional, and why the absence of "just transition" from the draft is a serious gap rather than a wording issue.

## 6. Posthumanism and the "end-user": a productive tension to be exploited, not smoothed over

The supervisor's theory and the client's terminology are in tension, and the tension is academically valuable.

- "End-user" is a **humanist, managerial and instrumental** category. It comes from product development and technology acceptance research; it positions a person by their function in relation to a technology, and it implies a designer/user binary.
- Posthumanism **dissolves exactly that binary**. Barad's intra-action denies that "designer" and "user" pre-exist their entanglement; Braidotti's nomadic subject resists functional reduction; Haraway's cyborg is precisely a figure that is not a clean "user".

A doctoral chapter that simply asserts "posthumanism supports end-user participation" will be weak, because posthumanism does not straightforwardly support user-centred design — it interrogates it. The stronger move, and the one I recommend for Chapter 3 §3.7, is to argue that posthumanism **reconstructs** the end-user: from a passive adopter of a finished artefact into a participant in a socio-material assemblage that includes the technology, the infrastructure, the dwelling, the fuel, the grid connection, the meter and the institution. On that reading, "end-user participation" is not a consultation exercise but a reconfiguration of the assemblage — and acceptance is an emergent property of that reconfiguration rather than a variable to be measured in a survey. This is a genuine theoretical contribution and it directly serves RQ2, RQ3 and RQ4.

**Warning to record:** this move changes what "acceptance" means. If acceptance becomes emergent and relational, then TAM (which treats acceptance as an individual intention formed from perceived usefulness and ease of use) becomes a *contrast* case rather than a supporting theory. Chapter 3 §3.9 must decide this explicitly rather than listing TAM as compatible.

## 7. Positioning within the literature

The study sits at the intersection of four bodies of work, and its claim to originality depends on the intersection being genuinely unoccupied:

1. **Energy poverty and energy security in informal settlements** (mostly quantitative, indicator-driven: MEPI, SEA, IEA tiers; South African work by Musango and colleagues).
2. **Living Labs and participatory innovation** (mostly European and ICT-origin; energy Living Labs are a recent and thin literature; African energy Living Labs are thinner still).
3. **Energy justice and just transition** (well developed conceptually; weakly connected to design practice and to informal settlements).
4. **Posthumanist philosophy** (well developed in education, geography, STS and feminist theory; almost entirely absent from energy engineering management).

The **gap** the thesis can defensibly claim is not "no one has studied energy poverty" (false) nor "no one has used a Living Lab for energy" (false — Mukama et al. 2022; Petrulaityte et al. 2022). It is: *no study has theorised, designed and evaluated a systemic Living Lab for energy innovation in a South African informal settlement using a posthumanist ontology of distributed agency, with energy acceptance as the organising construct and just-transition outcomes as the evaluative criterion.* That claim is narrower, defensible and testable. See `research_gaps.md`.

## 8. Scope boundaries that need to be fixed

| Boundary | Current state | Decision required |
|---|---|---|
| **Geographic** | South Africa (Tzaneen) + Nigeria (Umuahia) | Retain only South Africa, or justify the second site as a contrast case |
| **Technology** | Biogas digester (pilot) | Confirm; biogas is defensible for cooking energy but must be justified against solar PV, LPG and improved cookstoves |
| **Unit of analysis** | Household? End-user? Living Lab? Socio-technical assemblage? | Must be stated; posthumanism pushes toward the assemblage |
| **Level of analysis** | Household / community / municipal / national | Currently oscillates; recommendation: household-community with institutional context |
| **Temporal** | Fieldwork reported for 2022–2023; plan for 2027–2029 | Must be resolved before the abstract and timeline |
| **Epistemic status** | Proposal vs completed study | Determines whether the abstract reports findings or expected contributions |

## 9. Terminology control list (for Phase 5)

The following inconsistencies must be standardised across the thesis:

| Use | Do not use | Note |
|---|---|---|
| end-user(s) | end user, enduser, user (when end-user is meant) | Hyphenate consistently |
| Systems Living Lab (SLL) | systems living lab, Systems living lab, SETI Living Lab | Define once, then abbreviate |
| SETILL | SETI, SETI LL, GETI | One acronym only |
| energy poverty | energy insecurity (when poverty is meant) | The draft conflates them; define both and state the relationship |
| acceptance | adoption, uptake, take-up, acceptability | Four distinct constructs; define and use consistently |
| just energy transition | energy transition (when justice is meant) | New construct for this study |
| energy justice | social justice (when energy-specific) | New construct |
| South African informal settlement | poor informal settlement, informal settlement (alone) | Keep the national context visible |
| systems thinking | systemic thinking, system thinking | Consistent form |
| co-design / co-creation / co-research / prototyping / testing | co designing, co-creating (inconsistent hyphenation) | Fixed list from the RQs |
