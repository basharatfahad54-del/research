# Terminology Change Log

**Purpose:** record the shift from the old concept note (D-01) to the current proposal,
so that the change is deliberate, defensible and visible to the supervisor.
**Basis:** word counts computed over the extracted D-01 text (`ch1_dump.txt` to
`ch4_dump.txt`, ≈68,400 words) and over the new chapter drafts.

---

## 1. Why terminology had to change

The brief fixes the study as a **Systemic Living Lab for Just Energy Transition** in an
informal settlement in **Tzaneen**, focused on **community-scale biogas**, with
**critical posthumanism** as the primary theory. The old concept note was written to a
different emphasis. It is not wrong, but leaving its vocabulary in place would make the
proposal answer a question the candidate is no longer asking. Three problems drove the
change:

1. **The organising lens.** D-01 is gender-centred. That is a legitimate body of
   literature, but it is not the theory the brief selects.
2. **Geographic contamination.** D-01 mixes Tzaneen material with material on Umuahia,
   Nigeria, and with wind and solar IPP material that does not belong to a
   community-scale biogas study.
3. **Claims outrunning evidence.** D-01 uses past tense for fieldwork that has not
   happened, and asserts findings that no data support.

## 3. Term-by-term changes

| # | Old term / usage (D-01) | New term / usage | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "women" as the primary unit of analysis | "end-users" and "household members" | The brief's unit is the end-user within a socio-material configuration. Gender remains an axis of difference, not the frame. |
| 2 | "gender mainstreaming" as the aim | "just transition" as the aim, with recognition as one dimension | Justice is the normative goal; gender is one form of difference that recognition must hold. |
| 3 | "community" as an undifferentiated whole | "end-users", "participants", "stakeholders", with the settlement's internal differences named | Avoids treating the settlement as homogeneous and hides nothing about who is included. |
| 4 | "acceptance" as a decision or intention | "acceptance" as an emergent, provisional and contested stabilisation in use | Consistent with posthumanism and with the TAM critique in Chapter 3. |
| 5 | "technology adoption" | "sustained use" / "socio-material uptake" | Adoption language assumes a discrete decision and a passive user. |
| 6 | "beneficiaries" | "participants" / "end-users" | Beneficiary language implies a one-way transfer of benefit and obscures agency. |
| 7 | "the poor" / "the community" as object | "households", "residents", "participants" | Precise, non-deficit language. |
| 8 | "empowerment" used loosely | "agency" (distributed) and "potentia" (capacity to act) | Ties the term to the theory rather than to a slogan. |
| 9 | "implementation" | "co-design", "prototyping", "testing", "configuration" | Matches the Living Lab method and the posthumanist account of design. |
| 10 | "impact" | "outcomes", "effects", "proposed relationships" | "Impact" overclaims causality at proposal stage. |
| 11 | "will demonstrate" / "has shown" (future and past certainty) | "is proposed to", "the study will examine", "may" | Removes claims the study cannot yet support. |
| 12 | "Umuahia", "Nigeria" | "Tzaneen", "Limpopo", "South Africa" | Geographic correction. |
| 13 | "wind", "solar PV", "IPP" as focus | "community-scale biogas" as focus; national renewables mentioned only as context | Focus correction. |
| 14 | "System Energy Technology Innovation Living Lab" (old expansion) | "Systemic Living Lab for Just Energy Transition" (brief expansion) | See D8. A footnote must reconcile the two so the change is transparent. |

## 4. How the gender-and-energy literature should now be used

The nine verified sources from the Musango group (L13–L16, L47–L49) remain valuable.
They should be cited for **three specific purposes**, not as the organising frame:

1. **Recognition.** They document how energy interventions can fail to recognise the
   knowledge, labour and needs of particular household members.
2. **Participation.** They provide South African evidence on who is included and
   excluded from energy innovation processes.
3. **Living Lab design.** L14 and L16 are directly about living labs and gendered
   energy transitions, and therefore support the Living Lab component of SETILL.

They should **not** be used to claim that this study is a gender study. The correct
formulation is that SETILL treats gender as one axis of difference that a just
transition must recognise, while its primary theoretical commitment is critical
posthumanism.

## 5. Language and register

| Issue | Old | New |
|---|---|---|
| Certainty | "This research proves…" | "This study examines…"; "the framework proposes…" |
| Tense for fieldwork | Past tense ("interviews were conducted") | Future/proposal tense ("interviews will be conducted") |
| Agency of technology | "biogas will empower households" | "households and biogas systems may co-constitute new arrangements" |
| Deficit framing | "lack of awareness", "ignorance" | "information asymmetries", "conditions of participation" |
| Overclaiming novelty | "first study to…" | "this study contributes…"; claims limited to the case |

## 7. Note on D-01 citations

Several D-01 sources are cited in the new Chapter 3 only after being checked. Where the
original source could not be verified, the new draft does **not** reproduce the claim.
This is why the new draft is shorter and less confident than D-01: it claims less
because it has checked more.
