2. *Batinge et al.* The earlier draft omitted four authors. The correct author list is Batinge, Musango, Ceschin, Ambole, Smit and Petrulaityte, in *Systems Research and Behavioral Science*, 39(2), pp. 287–304.

6. *Oosthuizen et al.* The title was given in abbreviated form. The correct title is "Exploring factors that influence the mainstreaming of gendered energy interventions in poor urban environments: a structured literature review".

7. *Petrulaityte et al.* The author list and title were both incorrect. The correct citation is given in the reference list above.

8. *Walker and Day.* The DOI in the earlier draft resolved to a different article. The correct DOI is 10.1016/j.enpol.2012.01.044.

Two further corrections were made to the body of the text rather than to the reference list. The earlier draft described the literature review as a "stand-alone qualitative systematic literature review (realist review)" without reporting a protocol, search strings or screening counts. That description has been replaced with an accurate account of an integrative review. The draft also claimed that no study had ever combined Living Lab and systems approaches to address energy poverty in South Africa. That claim could not be sustained and has been narrowed.

## Appendix B: Draft interview guide

The guide is indicative. It will be piloted and revised before use.

**Opening**

- Thank you for making time. This conversation is about how energy is used in your household and what makes it difficult or easy. There are no right answers, and you can stop at any point or decline a question.

**Section 1: Current practice**

- Walk me through yesterday. What did you cook, and what did you use?
- Which fuels do you use in a normal week? Why those?
- Where does the fuel come from, and who fetches or buys it?
- What happens when the electricity goes off?

**Section 2: Cost and labour**

- Roughly how much does energy cost your household in a month? How does that compare with other expenses?
- Who pays, and how is the decision made?
- Who does the cooking, the collecting and the cleaning? Has that changed?

**Section 3: Failure and coping**

- Tell me about a time when the system did not work. What happened?
- What did you do instead? What did it cost?
- Is there anything you have stopped using? Why?

**Section 4: Decisions and voice**

- If a new energy system were installed here, who would decide how it is run?
- Have you been asked about a project before? What happened?
- Who speaks for the community in meetings? Do you feel represented?

**Section 5: Fairness**

- If a new system were installed, who do you think would benefit most? Who might be left out?
- What would make you say the project was fair?
- Is there anything a project like this should not do?

**Closing**

- Is there anything I should have asked and did not?
- Would you be willing to speak again?

## Appendix C: Consent summary

The consent form will be available in English and in the local language. The summary below states its content in plain terms.

*What this study is about.* The study is about how energy is used in this settlement and how a community-scale energy system could be designed with residents rather than for them.

*What participation involves.* An interview of about an hour, or a place in a focus group of about ninety minutes, or keeping a diary for four weeks, or taking part in a workshop. You will not be asked to do all of these unless you agree.

*Risks.* There are no physical risks beyond ordinary daily life. Some questions concern money, health and conflict, and may be uncomfortable. You may decline any question.

*Benefits.* There is no payment for participation except where compensation for time is stated. The study may produce a pilot system, but participation does not entitle you to a service.

*Confidentiality.* Your name will not appear in any publication. Recordings and notes will be stored securely and destroyed after five years. Photographs will be used only with your separate permission.

*Voluntary participation.* You may withdraw at any time, and you may ask for your data to be removed.

*Ethics approval.* The study has been approved by the Faculty of Engineering and the Built Environment Research Ethics Committee. The approval number will be inserted when it is issued.

*Contact.* The researcher and the supervisor's contact details are given on the form.

## Appendix E: Workshop protocol

Each workshop runs for three hours and follows the same structure.

1. *Welcome and purpose* (15 minutes). Restate what the workshop can and cannot decide.
2. *Ground rules* (10 minutes). Agreed by the group, including how disagreement will be handled.
3. *Evidence presentation* (30 minutes). Findings from the diagnostic phase, presented visually, with time for challenge.
4. *Small-group task* (60 minutes). Groups of five to seven, mixed by gender and tenure where possible, each with a facilitator and a visual template.
5. *Plenary* (45 minutes). Each group presents; disagreements are recorded on a flip chart rather than resolved.
6. *Decision record* (20 minutes). What was decided, what was deferred, who will do what.

A decision log is completed after each workshop. It records the decision, the alternatives considered, who supported and who opposed, and the reasons given. The log is a primary data source for C9 and for the procedural justice indicators.

## Appendix F: Glossary of theoretical terms

| Term | Plain-language definition |
|---|---|
| Agential cut | The boundary drawn within a phenomenon that separates what is observed from what observes; it is a decision with consequences. |
| Agential realism | Barad's ontology in which entities emerge from relations rather than preceding them. |
| Assemblage | A configuration of human and non-human elements whose relations produce capacities that none has alone. |
| Diffraction | Reading one account through another to see what each makes visible, rather than judging one against the other. |
| Distributed agency | The claim that the capacity to act is spread across people, artefacts, infrastructures and institutions. |
| Intra-action | The relation through which entities acquire their boundaries and properties; distinct from interaction between pre-existing entities. |
| Potestas | Braidotti's term for constraining, negating power. |
| Potentia | Braidotti's term for enabling, capacity-building power. |
| Response-ability | Haraway's term for the practised capacity to respond to others accountably. |
| Situated knowledge | Knowledge produced from a particular position, which can therefore be located and checked. |
| Sympoiesis | Making-with; collaboration as a condition of life rather than a technique. |
| Apparatus | The arrangement of practices, instruments and concepts through which a phenomenon is produced and observed. |
