### 5.5.8 Internet research

A final collection approach is desk-based: the structured use of internet resources for the institutional and documentary layer of the descriptive study. This is listed as a method of the study because a part of the evidence the design requires — municipal documents, policy instruments, the utility's public data on supply and tariffs, regulatory filings, and the documented history of prior projects in the municipality — exists only in that form, and because the study's epistemology requires that all sources of evidence about the configuration be named and treated comparably rather than leaving the documentary layer implicit.

The planned practice is conventional but explicit. Sources will be identified against the boundary statement (C1), so that the documentary collection covers the actors and systems the boundary names rather than whatever is easiest to find. Each class of source — municipal and provincial documents, national policy and planning instruments, utility and regulatory publications, and prior project reports — will be logged with its provenance, date and access route in the same evidence register as the field-generated material. Where documents disagree, the disagreement is treated as data about the institutional field rather than as noise to be resolved.

Two quality matters are handled alongside the trustworthiness strategies set out below. The first is currency: official web resources change, so every document is stored with its access date and a copy, and figures that depend on administrative data are tied to the release from which they were taken. The second is standing: documents are read as the products of positions, in the same way that interviews are — a municipal annual report is an account produced by one stakeholder in the configuration, and the analysis treats it as such. The documentary layer is triangulated against the field-generated evidence rather than being used as its arbiter.
