# 7. Research Plan and Timeline

## 7.1 Overview

The study is planned over 36 months, beginning in January 2027 and ending in December 2029, subject to registration and ethical clearance. The plan assumes full-time study. It includes contingency for the delays that fieldwork in this context reliably produces, and it front-loads the ethics application because that is the most common source of delay.

Figure 7.1 presents the timeline. The six phases overlap deliberately. Fieldwork and analysis overlap because transcription and coding begin while data collection continues; a study that waits for all data before beginning analysis loses the opportunity to adjust the instruments. Co-design and prototyping overlap because the specification is refined during construction.

## 7.2 Phase detail

### 7.2.1 Phase 1: Foundation and ethics (months 1–6)

The first phase establishes the conditions for the study. It includes a literature update to catch work published since the proposal, the development of the interview and workshop instruments, the ethics application and the site scoping.

The ethics application will be submitted in month 3, and the study assumes a three-month review period. If clearance takes longer, the scoping work can continue because it involves no data collection, but the fieldwork start will slip.

The site selection memorandum will be completed by month 6. It will assess at least three candidate settlements against the criteria in Table 5.1 and will state the reasons for the choice.

The deliverables are an approved proposal, an ethics certificate, a set of instruments and a site selection memorandum.

### 7.2.2 Phase 2: Descriptive study I (months 7–14)

The second phase produces the diagnostic account. It begins with key-informant interviews, which establish the institutional context and identify the households and groups to approach. Focus groups follow, then transect walks, then diaries, then photovoice. The sequence is not rigid, but it is deliberate: the earlier methods inform the later ones.

Twelve to fifteen households will keep diaries for four weeks. Ten to twelve residents will take part in photovoice. Twenty to twenty-five household interviews and eight to ten interviews with non-users will be conducted. The exact numbers will be adjusted as saturation is assessed.

Transcription begins in month 9 and continues through the phase. Coding begins in month 10.

The deliverables are a site report, a diagnostic account organised around the ten components, and a validated coding frame.

### 7.2.3 Phase 3: Analysis and modelling (months 15–21)

The third phase consolidates the analysis. It produces the situational maps, the causal loop diagrams and the first revision of the framework. It also produces a draft of the findings chapter, which is submitted to the supervisor for review.

This phase includes a member-checking session with participants, in which the emerging findings are presented and discussed. The session is not a validation exercise in the positivist sense; it is an opportunity for participants to disagree, and the disagreements are recorded.

The deliverables are a findings chapter draft, a set of maps and diagrams, and a revised framework.

### 7.2.4 Phase 4: Co-design and prototyping (months 22–27)

The fourth phase is the design intervention. Four workshops produce the service model, the governance agreement, the technical specification and the evaluation plan. Construction begins after the specification is agreed, and is undertaken by a local contractor with the technology team, using local labour where possible.

Commissioning includes operator training and a maintenance schedule. The phase ends with a working prototype, a signed governance agreement and a trained operator group.

This phase carries the highest risk in the plan. Construction can be delayed by weather, by supply, by permission and by disagreement. A two-month contingency is built into the schedule.

The deliverables are an agreed specification, a working prototype, a governance agreement and a trained operator group.

### 7.2.5 Phase 5: Evaluation and drafting (months 28–33)

The fifth phase assesses the intervention and writes the thesis. Monitoring data are collected over six months: gas output, water use, feedstock input, maintenance events, cost and use by household. Follow-up interviews are conducted with the original participants and with a sample of households that did not take part.

The justice profile is constructed and discussed with participants before it is finalised. The thesis is drafted, and a paper is prepared for a conference or journal.

The deliverables are a justice profile, a full thesis draft and a conference paper.

### 7.2.6 Phase 6: Finalisation (months 34–36)

The final phase completes the thesis, prepares the examination copy and submits. It also includes the exit handover: a community feedback session, the transfer of records and contacts, and the establishment of the maintenance arrangement agreed in Phase 4.

The deliverables are a submitted thesis and a completed handover.

## 7.3 Milestones and decision points

Five decision points are built into the plan. Each is a point at which the study could be adjusted.

*Month 6: site confirmation.* If no site meets the criteria, the study will either relax a criterion with documented reasons or delay the fieldwork by three months while a new site is scoped.

*Month 14: framework revision.* If the diagnostic evidence does not support the framework, the framework is revised before the co-design phase. This is the point at which a major revision is cheapest.

*Month 22: go/no-go on construction.* Construction proceeds only if a governance agreement and a maintenance arrangement are in place. If they are not, the study will run a design-only intervention and report the reasons, which is itself a finding about the conditions for community-scale technology.

*Month 27: prototype acceptance.* If the system does not perform to specification, the study will document the shortfall and analyse it as evidence about the configuration.

*Month 33: submission readiness.* If the draft is incomplete, a three-month extension will be requested.

## 7.4 Resources

The study requires the following resources, in addition to the researcher's time and the supervisor's guidance.

Fieldwork costs include transport between Johannesburg and Tzaneen, accommodation during fieldwork blocks, participant compensation for time, facilitator fees, transcription and translation, and printing of instruments.

Equipment costs include biogas system components, a gas stove, plumbing and safety fittings, and monitoring instruments.

Software includes NVivo or an equivalent qualitative analysis package, and Vensim or an equivalent system dynamics tool for causal loop diagrams.

The study will seek funding from the university's postgraduate research budget, from the research programme in which it is located, and from external funders where appropriate. A detailed budget is provided separately to the school.

## 7.5 Supervision and progress management

The study will be supervised by Dr K. Aboalez, with co-supervision where the school requires it. Progress will be managed through monthly supervision meetings, a written progress report every six months, and an annual review with the school's higher degrees committee.

A research journal will be maintained, recording decisions, problems and reflections. The journal is both a management tool and a source of evidence for the reflexive component of the study.

Risk will be reviewed at each supervision meeting against the register in Table 5.7.
