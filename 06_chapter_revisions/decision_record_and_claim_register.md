## 2. What D2 changes

D2 is not a filing decision. It determines whether the thesis is a plan or a report, and it invalidates a substantial body of text that currently reads as completed research.

**Three categories of text are affected:**

**Category A — Fabricated empirical findings.** Passages that state, in the past tense, that data were collected, participants recruited, workshops held, instruments administered and saturation reached. There is no data, no ethics clearance and no instrument behind any of these. If submitted as written, this is research misconduct, not a drafting error. **All must be deleted**, not softened.

**Category B — Case-site contamination.** The design is written around two sites (Tzaneen and Umuahia). D6 permits one. The comparative logic, the 50-household figure and the Nigeria material must go.

**Category C — Tense and framing errors.** Legitimate methodological *plans* written in the past tense. These survive, converted to future/intended tense and marked as proposed.

## 4. What survives D2 intact

D2 removes the findings, not the scholarship. The following are unaffected and remain usable:

- The literature review's **source base** — though its argument, length and RQ alignment must be rebuilt (D4).
- The **GENS lineage** — Musango, Mukama, Anditi, Petrulaityte, Batinge, Ceschin, Smit. All carry verified DOIs.
- The **problems statement, context and rationale** — energy poverty in South African informal settlements is a real, well-documented problem that requires no primary data to establish.
- The **Living Lab and co-design methods** as *design*, drawn from Petrulaityte et al. (2022) and the GENS toolkit.
- The **biogas technology rationale**, once recast as proposed.
- The **systems modelling** component, as a planned analytical strategy.

A proposal does not need results. It needs a defensible problem, a theory, a design and a plan. Under D2 the thesis is shorter in one sense and far stronger in another: nothing has to be invented.

## 5. Immediate sequencing consequences
