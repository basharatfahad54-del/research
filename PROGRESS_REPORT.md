## 5. Ethical and integrity issues that must be raised with the supervisor

These are stated plainly because they affect whether the work can be submitted at all.

### 5.1 The institutional AI prohibition

D-04 (the programme's own proposal guideline) states: *"It is prohibited to use AI tools to generate or rephrase text."* The client's brief asks for AI-assisted drafting of Chapter 3, revision of chapters, an abstract, figures and reference management.

These two positions are in direct conflict. I will not resolve it unilaterally. The options are:

1. **Disclose and seek permission.** Ask the supervisor and faculty whether AI assistance for drafting, editing and reference formatting is permitted with declaration. Many universities now allow assisted editing with disclosure while prohibiting generated text.
2. **Restrict the tool to non-generative functions.** Use it for document analysis, structure, gap mapping, reference verification, formatting and consistency checking — all of which are legitimate research-support activities — and have the researcher write all thesis prose.
3. **Use drafts strictly as scaffolding.** Any prose produced is treated as an outline that the researcher rewrites, substantiates and takes ownership of, with sources read first-hand.

**Recommendation:** obtain a written position from the supervisor before Phase 3, and record the decision in this folder. Proceeding without it risks the degree.

### 5.2 The Turnitin request

The brief asks for "Turnitin of less than 5%" and "AI 0%" with reports. This cannot be delivered by an AI system, for three reasons:

- Turnitin scores depend on the comparison corpus, the document's length and the accepted-quote settings; they cannot be predicted or guaranteed.
- The thesis currently has **no reference list**, so a similarity check would flag every uncited source and produce a high score that reflects citation failure, not copying.
- AI-detection scores are probabilistic and not designed to certify authorship.

**What can be delivered instead** (Phase 8):
- An **originality and citation audit**: every paragraph checked for unattributed borrowing, every quotation marked and page-referenced, every citation matched to a source.
- A **similarity-risk register** listing passages likely to match (definitions, standard method descriptions, quoted passages).
- Instructions for the researcher to run the **official Turnitin report through UJ's licensed account**, which is the only valid report for submission.

No Turnitin report will be fabricated.

### 5.3 The gender removal instruction

See `research_topic_analysis.md` §5. Removing 505 instances of "gender" and 335 of "women" is not an editorial operation; it dismantles the draft's explanatory mechanism. The three-tier strategy proposed there preserves academic integrity while meeting the client's intent. **It requires supervisor approval before execution.**

### 5.4 The reported fieldwork

**Resolved under D2: no fieldwork has been conducted.** The draft nevertheless reports surveys (August–September 2022), ethnography (July 2023), a co-design workshop in Tzaneen, a 42-participant group design and biogas testing — all in the past tense, with specific counts and a saturation claim. Fourteen passages require deletion or substantive rewrite; the full register is in `06_chapter_revisions/decision_record_and_claim_register.md` §3. The 2027–2029 Gantt chart is the only part of the empirical material consistent with reality.

## 7. Outstanding academic issues

1. **Theory–method incoherence.** A posthumanist ontology (relational, distributed agency) sits uneasily with a constructivist-interpretivist epistemology centred on individual meaning-making. This must be resolved in Chapter 5 or the thesis will be internally inconsistent.
2. **The "end-user" construct needs theorising.** It is currently a managerial category imported without critique; Chapter 3 §3.7 must reconstruct it (see topic analysis §6).
3. **Acceptance is defined five ways.** Acceptance, adoption, uptake, take-up and acceptability are used interchangeably. RQ3 cannot be answered until one construct is defined.
4. **The novelty claim is overstated.** The GENS programme (2019–2022) already produced gendered energy Living Labs, a co-design toolkit, a gender analysis framework and systems modelling. The present study's novelty must be relocated to posthumanist theory + just transition + acceptance + South African settlement focus.
5. **"Just transition" and "energy justice" are entirely absent** from a thesis whose title contains "Just Energy Transition". This is the largest single conceptual gap.
6. **The literature review is 35,163 words** and functions as a catalogue. It must become an argument at roughly half the length.
7. **No reference list.** This is not a formatting issue; it means no claim in the document can currently be checked.
8. **Front matter is non-functional** (broken TOC, blank abstract, incomplete figure/table/acronym lists).
9. **Figure quality.** Figures 3.1–3.5 are raster images of variable legibility, with no source notes; they cannot be reused in a submitted thesis.
10. **Terminology drift** (SETILL/SETI/GETI; rural/urban informal settlement) undermines the claim to a single coherent model.
