ABSTRACT: WRITING PLAN (NOT THE FINAL ABSTRACT)

STATUS AND PURPOSE

The school's proposal structure (Structure of a Proposal in Engineering
Management, items 2 and 12) states that the abstract is 250-300 words and is
written last. This file is therefore a plan and a set of drafting instructions,
not a finished abstract. The final text should be written after Chapters 1-9
are stable, because every sentence in the abstract must be traceable to a
chapter.

The school requires four elements, in this order:
(1) research problem
(2) objectives
(3) methodology
(4) expected contributions

Research problem          55-65 words

CONTENT PLAN BY ELEMENT

1. RESEARCH PROBLEM (55-65 words)
   - One sentence on the setting: South African informal settlement, household
     energy insecurity.
   - One sentence on the gap: energy innovations are designed with limited
     direct involvement of end-users, and participation is weak or tokenistic.
   - One sentence on the consequence: this reduces justice and end-user agency
     and undermines energy security.
   - Do not name Tzaneen or biogas here unless the school template requires it.
   - [CANDIDATE TO ADD] one concrete local observation that makes the problem
     visible (for example, a pattern you have seen in household energy use).

2. OBJECTIVES (45-55 words)
   - State the aim: to advance theoretical and empirical knowledge of energy
     technology innovation through a systems-thinking Living Lab that places
     acceptance at the centre of design, prototyping and testing.
   - Then compress the five research questions into one sentence of purpose,
     not five sentences. The RQs are already in Chapter 1.
   - Use "end-users" or "household members"; do not use "men", "women",
     "households headed by women", or "gender" in the abstract.

3. METHODOLOGY (90-110 words)
   - Paradigm: posthumanist-informed, qualitative-dominant.
   - Strategy: single case (Tzaneen informal settlement), community-scale
     biogas as the technology focus, embedded in a Systemic Living Lab.
   - Design: exploratory sequential, with a co-design and prototyping phase.
   - Data: semi-structured interviews, focus groups or co-design workshops,
     observation, documents, and prototyping or testing sessions.
   - Analysis: thematic analysis supported by system dynamics or causal loop
     modelling.
   - Sampling: purposive and snowball, with saturation.
   - State that the study is a proposal, so methods are proposed, not reported.
   - Do not report participant numbers unless the final methodology chapter
     fixes them. Mark any number [VERIFY] against Chapter 5.

4. EXPECTED CONTRIBUTIONS (55-75 words)
   - Theoretical: extends critical posthumanism from engineering education into
     household energy innovation in an informal settlement.
   - Practical: a tested SETILL model and participation mechanisms that
     designers and municipalities can use.
   - Policy: evidence on acceptance and justice for local energy planning.
   - Methodological: a systemic Living Lab protocol suitable for low-income
     settings.
   - One sentence on the boundary: findings will be analytically, not
     statistically, generalisable.

DRAFTING RULES FOR THE ABSTRACT

- Write in the candidate's voice, plain academic English, no stock phrases.
- One idea per sentence; no sentence longer than about 30 words.
- No citations in the abstract.
- No forward promises ("this study will revolutionise") and no claims of
  results that do not exist.
- No claim about Turnitin or AI-detection scores.
- Keep acronyms to SETILL and, if necessary, RQ; expand at first use.
- Check the final word count in Word, not by eye.
